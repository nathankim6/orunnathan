"It's not a gradual drift but rather a bias that emerges almost immediately," Echeverría-Huarte said. The researchers wondered whether the behavior might be emerging collectively, similar to how pedestrians split into two opposite-moving lanes on crowded sidewalks. But when participants were tested alone, 75% still moved counterclockwise, suggesting that the tendency is individual. Were there cultural factors behind the preference? Pedestrians typically walk on the same side as cars drive on in their country, and they also usually move in that direction when they meet an obstacle. So the team ran experiments in Japan, where pedestrian lanes form on the left. Although the researchers were convinced they would see the opposite pattern to the one in Spain, they once again observed participants moving counterclockwise. Finally, they wondered if some unacknowledged social convention drilled into adults, but not yet present in children, might be at play. They contacted researchers who had conducted a study in a Japanese kindergarten in which 52 children moved at random while music played. The videos of the children revealed that most of them also moved counterclockwise.

Enrico Ronchi, who models emergency evacuations at Lund University in Sweden and was not involved in the research, said the findings "open up many new, interesting avenues in the field of crowd dynamics." He would like to know, for example, whether the counterclockwise bias holds up among people with disabilities or during emergency evacuations. Karol Bacik, an applied mathematician at MIT who was also not involved in the research, said the finding challenges our understanding of human locomotion. "The counterclockwise bias may have far-reaching consequences for everyday pedestrian traffic, but we simply have not looked for them yet," he said. Zuriguel and Echeverría-Huarte next plan to investigate new hypotheses using biomechanics, virtual reality, neuroscience or even animal behavior. "Fish are the canonical animal that move in circles," Zuriguel said. "But whether they prefer rotating in one direction or the other, I don't know."

**27. ⑤** — give ~ pause = "잠시 멈춰 생각하게 하다". 꽃가루 노출을 줄이려면 앞으로 어떤 나무를 심을지 신중히 골라야 한다(뒤의 설탕단풍·목련 이야기와 이어진다).
왜: 에두른 표현이 핵심을 담는 자리의 함의 — 기말 23 · 27.

**28. ①** — 기온·강수를 모델에 넣어 밝은 밤하늘의 효과를 **따로(independent)** 보여 주었다 — 바로 뒤에 "빛 공해가 기온보다 영향이 크다"는 발견과 일치한다고 했으므로 little effect 는 정반대다(원문 *had independent effects*).
왜: 이어 붙인 뉴스 지문에 반의어 하나를 심는 어휘 — 기말 28(less → more competition).
